### Youth of May

**Synopsis**
Youth of May is a poignant South Korean drama set against the backdrop of the 1980 Gwangju Uprising, focusing on the love story between medical student Hwang Hee-tae and nurse Kim Myung-hee.

**Cast**
- Lee Do-Hyun
- Go Min-Si
- Lee Sang-Yi
- Keum Sae-Rok

**Review**
As a college student who loves both history and K-dramas, Youth of May hit me right in the feels. Set during the Gwangju Uprising in South Korea in May 1980, this 12-episode drama blends a tender love story with a powerful historical backdrop, making it both emotionally moving and educational.

The story follows Hwang Hee-tae, a brilliant but emotionally scarred medical student, and Kim Myung-hee, a strong-willed nurse who dreams of studying abroad. They meet under unusual circumstances—Myung-hee goes on a blind date pretending to be her friend—and what starts as a fake setup turns into a deep and genuine connection. Their chemistry is undeniable, and watching their relationship grow is both heartwarming and bittersweet.

What really stood out to me was how the drama didn’t shy away from portraying the brutal reality of the Gwangju Uprising. It’s not just a love story—it’s a reminder of the sacrifices made by young people who stood up for democracy. The contrast between the innocence of first love and the violence of political unrest is jarring, but it’s what makes the show so impactful.

The acting was phenomenal. Lee Do-hyun and Go Min-si brought so much depth to their characters. You could really feel their pain, joy, and desperation. The cinematography and soundtrack also added to the emotional weight of the story.

As a student, I found Youth of May incredibly inspiring. It made me reflect on how young people like me once had to fight for basic rights and freedom. It’s a drama that stays with you long after the credits roll.
